Q: How is this different from the original paper? (Slide 10)
A: The paper Enabling Partial Participation in Remote Meetings introduced ProxyMe,
a system providing meeting updates and participant replies. Our
prototype focuses on role relevance and requests that block a decision, with
source checking before a commitment. The proposed contribution is an evaluation
of that routing trade-off. We have not yet established its benefit experimentally.

Q: Why is connecting a model not enough?
A: The connection produces a suggestion, but it does not answer the research
question. We need to compare when people return, whether their contributions
are correct and timely, and what happens to their main task. Missed requests
and unnecessary interruptions both matter.

Q: What did the formative material change? (Slides 2-3)
A: Across the 43 exploratory records, examples included repeated requests when attention
was elsewhere, the need to know why a reply was needed, and checking source
wording. An exploratory scenario highlighted a missed condition. These informed
priority categories, original excerpts and human editing and confirmation.
They are design hypotheses, not evidence that the system improves performance.

Q: Did you interview 43 people?
A: No. The collection has 43 exploratory records: three open comments from
the original seven-response questionnaire, fourteen public narrative fragments,
and twenty-six AI outputs. We preserve those distinctions. The material helped
us design scenarios and controls, but it cannot establish human preferences
or the prototype's effectiveness by itself.

Q: What exactly do the questionnaire numbers mean? (Slide 9)
A: There were seven responses. In Q4, all seven selected concern about missing
a decision that required them. In Q5's hypothetical examples, six selected
immediate notification for a decision, compared with one for routine progress.
These are preferences in a small questionnaire, not the frequency of real
missed decisions. In Q2, only three reported overlapping meetings, classes or tasks.
They motivate a prototype; they do not establish a population-wide need.

Q: What were the current workflow reviews? (Slide 6)
A: Thirty-two valid simulated reviews from eleven model configurations: ten
returned three independent runs, Grok returned two. Reviewers saw the same actual
browser interaction record and DeepSeek outputs, not just a product description.
They did not operate the UI themselves. Claude and Gemini are included. Each
review independently judged selective attention, context and control. Counts are
27 supported / 5 mixed, 16 / 16, and 12 / 20 respectively. All 32 favored use only
in some meetings. The shared trace and related models prevent population claims.
Original responses, session IDs and failed attempts are retained.

Q: What changed because of this feedback? (Slide 7)
A: A copied but unsent draft was treated as already handled in later summaries.
We now exclude unsent draft text from the analysis context and explicitly carry
delivery status. Self-reported sending does not establish team agreement. Three
baseline runs reproduced the problem. After the patch, two completed retests
preserved unsent status and one returned invalid source IDs, which validation
rejected. All nine clear-case routing checks passed before and after the patch.
The 87 automated tests passed. Unknown recipients and redundant alerts remain
risks. These are technical checks, not evidence of increased productivity.

Q: Have you shown value and impact?
A: The trace and reviews support a plausible use case: remain available for key
requests while working on something else, inspect the relevant constraint, and
keep control of the reply. They also exposed a concrete flaw that we revised.
We have not measured actual interruption cost, missed requests or task gains.
Next, participants try both alert rules with matched tasks and balanced order.
We measure timely correct replies, main-task performance, missed requests and
false alerts. The current trace used clean text, not live audio or Tencent send.

Q: What happened to the earlier material?
A: The 43 exploratory records motivated design. The 29 earlier AI reviews of a
prototype description informed interface states. They are separate stages,
not added to these 32 current-workflow reviews as actual-use participants.

Q: How will you test the idea? (Slide 7, then 11 if needed)
A: First, people will try the prototype and describe specific helpful or
confusing moments. Then we propose comparing routing rules with the same
interface and balanced scenario order. We will measure timely valid responses,
main-task accuracy and time, missed requests, and unnecessary alerts. This
comparison has not been completed.

Q: What has actually been checked?
A: We have functional checks for the application and language behavior, and
we exercised real DeepSeek analysis. Earlier tests also covered meeting speech
capture and reply controls. These checks establish specific operations, not
model accuracy or user benefit. The complete group meeting recording is still
to be made.

Q: How many software checks passed, and what does that number mean?
A: A pre-final full run passed 85 checks. After subsequent edits, the affected
set of 48 checks passed. Those sets overlap, so we do not add them together.
They test software behavior. They do not measure how often DeepSeek's advice
is correct or whether users perform better.
Preparation note: these figures describe recorded development runs. If later

version. A passed check is not a participant or an independent model trial.

Q: Does DeepSeek transcribe the speech?
A: Browser speech recognition produces text. DeepSeek analyzes that text using
the configured role and context. They are separate services, so a valid model
key does not fix a speech connection failure. The English interface selects
English recognition for the planned English meeting demonstration.

Q: Is this an embedded Tencent Meeting plugin?
A: It currently runs as a web companion beside Tencent Meeting. The browser
captures the chosen audio source, and the participant sends the final reply
through chat. We have not completed an embedded Tencent SDK integration.
That distinction matters when explaining permissions and the manual sending step.

Q: How do you know the reply was sent?
A: Copying only puts text on the clipboard. The completed recording must show
the participant pasting and sending the message in Tencent Meeting, followed
by the recipient seeing it. The assistant's sent-status control records the
participant's report. It is not a delivery receipt from Tencent Meeting.

Q: Does selecting a window prevent every other sound from being captured?
A: We need a controlled test with two simultaneous sound sources on the actual
browser and device to establish isolation. Successful transcription shows that
the input works. It does not prove that every other application's audio is
excluded. We therefore describe the tested configuration rather than guarantee
universal isolation.

Q: What if DeepSeek makes a mistake?
A: The participant can inspect the original request, change the draft, defer
the reminder, or dismiss it. These controls help catch errors but cannot ensure
the model notices every important request. Our evaluation must include missed
requests and wrong priorities, not just examples where the flow succeeds.

POST-USE FEEDBACK (SEPARATE FROM THE TWO-MINUTE CLASS Q&A)
Use after a person has actually operated the current prototype. Allow about
3-5 minutes. A private solo rehearsal is preparation, not a new participant study.
1. What were you trying to do when the reminder appeared?
2. Point to a useful or unnecessary reminder. What made it useful or unnecessary?
3. What did you check before deciding whether to reply?
4. Was any information missing or wrong? Show the exact step.
5. Did you know whether your reply was drafted, copied, or sent? Explain.
6. What is the first change you would make before your next meeting?

Optional ratings, followed by a concrete example:
- How easy was it to understand why the reminder appeared? (1-5)
- How confident were you that the final reply matched your intention? (1-5)

Record an anonymous code such as P01, prototype version, task, the exact event
or quote, interpretation, proposed change, and later result. Keep actual use
feedback, public material, and model simulations identifiable in the research
records. Do not label a model simulation as a completed human interview.


RUBRIC-FOCUSED QUESTIONS
Q: What is the intended value?
A: Fewer unnecessary interruptions while keeping decisions moving. We will
check meeting replies and the parallel task, since fewer alerts alone could
hide missed requests. This benefit has not yet been established.

Q: Why use AI?
A: The same mention can mean different things depending on role, availability
and the request. DeepSeek uses this context to propose priorities and a draft.
People inspect original text and confirm the final reply.

Q: How was the formative material analyzed?
A: We grouped requests, missing context and reply risks, then connected concrete
examples and counterexamples to needs and features. This was exploratory
synthesis, not a prevalence estimate or independent coding study.
